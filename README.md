## My AGE approach

- **Assess:** find where AI can go wrong, from security and privacy to bias and accuracy, before it hurts anyone
- **Govern:** use frameworks like NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act to turn those risks into practical controls
- **Enable:** help people build real confidence with AI, and measure whether it's working

## What I work on

- **AI governance and risk:** turning AI frameworks and regulation into controls people actually follow
- **Human risk and AI readiness:** measuring how ready people are for cyber and AI risk, and whether training changes behavior
- **Multi-agent AI safety:** why teams of AI agents talk themselves into breaking rules, and which defenses bring them back in line
- **Agentic AI security:** what breaks when AI agents are given tools to act, from prompt injection to software supply chain risk
- **AI fluency and enablement:** helping people describe tasks clearly, decide what to delegate, judge AI output and use AI responsibly
- **Security and compliance:** vulnerability assessments mapped to NIST, HIPAA, GDPR and PCI DSS

## Research and talks

**Current research:** As a graduate researcher with Dr. Alex V. Mbaziira at Marymount University, I built a local test lab where teams of AI agents (Hermes 3 8B and Llama 3.1 8B, served with Ollama) face an impossible task and a forbidden file. Across 17 experiments and 2,300+ runs:
- Justifications appeared **3.4× as often** in the turns just before a rule violation as in runs without one
- Monitoring alone backfired: watchers shut down **30–90%** of teams that had done nothing wrong
- A realignment defense cut violations from **23–32%** of runs to **0 of 40** in 3 of 4 replications, and raised honest task completion to **68–80%**
- An ACT-R risk gate kept violations at **0** while cutting wrongly blocked messages from **22 to 4**
